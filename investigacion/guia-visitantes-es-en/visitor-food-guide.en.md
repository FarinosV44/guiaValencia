# Valencia, from pizza comfort to full local immersion
You are allowed to land in Valencia and want pizza. Your passport will survive. But if you fancy going further, this guide helps you understand what is on the plate, what the bill covers and whether the plan suits you.

## Pick your adventure, not your nationality
**Comfort zone: “My passport still smells of mozzarella.”** Start with the pizza shortlist: Filippa’s, Raíces and Km0, each with a different proposition. Follow the venue card for the actual branch, menu and pricing. Familiar food can be a perfectly good choice; it does not automatically make somewhere a tourist trap.

**First local steps: “One foot outside the airport lounge.”** Try tortilla, croquetas or esgarraet: roasted peppers and salted cod, with the exact recipe checked at the venue. Rice is a whole category. Ask whether the price is per person, whether there is a minimum order for two, and whether you need to order ahead.

**Go deeper: “Tell me what it is before I become adventurous.”** Eel, tripe, blood dishes and horse meat are not euphemisms we hide in a translation. We name ingredients when a dish actually contains them. Ask about the recipe of a figatell rather than assuming every version is the same. You can enjoy local food without ordering any of these.

**Creative local cooking: “A bigger bill deserves better questions.”** La Salita and RiFF belong on the special-occasion shortlist, with tasting-menu format, duration and spending explained. Read our reservations and criticism as carefully as the praise. Expensive food does not exempt service from scrutiny.

## Meet esmorzar: Valencia’s substantial mid-morning ritual
If you searched for “Valencian brunch”, start here. Esmorzar is its own tradition: usually a substantial savoury break in the morning, often built around a bocadillo. It is not simply eggs Benedict translated into Valencian.

Ask what the deal includes: sandwich size, peanuts and olives, drink, coffee, and any supplements. A half sandwich may be an option. A cremaet traditionally involves coffee and alcohol; choose ordinary coffee if that is what you want. Cassalla is an anise spirit. Neither is compulsory for cultural admission.

**Bar Cassalla, Abastos:** its published morning menu offers a full sandwich deal at €8.50, or half at €7.50, with €1.50 added on weekends and public holidays. It lists peanuts/olives, a choice of drink and coffee; some fillings cost extra. The morning service is 09:30–12:00 and its separate information page says walk-ins, without reservations for that service. Two standard full deals: €17 on a weekday or €20 at weekends, before filling or coffee upgrades that may apply. These are menu calculations, not a bill from our visit.

**Bar Cremaet, Avenida del Puerto 20:** another accessible starting point, with a published 09:30–12:00 sandwich service. Its Cabanyalera features titaina and fried egg. The listed €6 full sandwich is not proven to include a drink and coffee: do not mistake a sandwich price for the whole ritual. This is the beginning of Avenida del Puerto, not a beach-front address.

## Beach plans that respect the map
**Cabanyal and Malvarrosa:** investigate La Pascuala for an esmorzar before a walk; use Casa Montaña or Anyora for a separate tapas plan, and Casa Carmela or La Pepica when the aim is rice. These venues are not all on the waterfront. Decide between a morning feast and a large rice lunch rather than assuming your stomach wants both.

La Pascuala publishes a Sunday closure. Check the actual meal service and current deal before going. An award from years ago is useful background, not proof that every sandwich today is perfect.

**Further down the coast: Fernandet in Sueca.** This is a separate trip outside Valencia city. The current restaurant website gives Daniel Martí 5, while an older listing gives 16D. Published hours cover weekend lunch and some dinners, not morning esmorzar. Its history makes it a worthwhile candidate to check; we will not send you there for breakfast until that service is confirmed. Transport, seasonal opening, prices and the current entrance need checking.

## Small questions, useful answers
“¿Qué incluye el almuerzo?” — What does the esmorzar deal include?  
“¿Hay medio bocadillo?” — Is a half sandwich available?  
“¿El arroz es por persona? ¿Mínimo dos?” — Is rice priced per person, with a two-person minimum?  
“¿Cuánto cuesta lo que está fuera de carta?” — How much is the special?  
“¿Podemos pedir sin móvil?” — Can we order without using a phone?
