When will Valencia fill up with Korean barbecues? They’ve already lit the grill

When you sit down to dinner and are handed tongs, the employment relationship gets a little ambiguous. You came to eat; the restaurant thinks you have kitchen potential. The useful question is what you pay, what is included and whether you fancy joining in.

Counting openings isn’t our job; counting what you pay and what’s included is. Here are the two ways to do it today: à la carte in the city or a buffet on the outskirts.

À la carte in the city, buffet outside it

Éter Artes y Ciencias

à la carte Korean barbecue, City of Arts and Sciences

Trying Korean barbecue without the buffet blow-out: order the portions you want and grill them at the table.

The catch: An all-you-can-eat dinner: it is à la carte. Nor a serious conversation while minding the pork belly.

€16 per person, partial total

Hankook Alfafar

Korean barbecue buffet, Alfafar (outside Valencia city)

Documentary researchOutside Valencia city

Hungry groups who enjoy grilling in rounds: tabletop grill plus sushi, at a buffet price.

The catch: Light eaters, or anyone wanting one specific dish: unlimited access adds little.

€18.90 per person, base price

Buffet or à la carte: buy the plan you want

If you want one specific dish, unlimited access adds little. If you enjoy cooking in rounds and trying several meats, look into the buffet: time limit, help with the grill, cuts included and supplements. Industrial décor does not count as a portion.

On a date, cooking together can spark conversation; it also takes attention. With a baby or a child who keeps getting up, check the layout and table first: no photo proves the grill suits that plan.

The metamorphosis of the endless menu

Our cartoon goes from the classic Chinese restaurant to the wok bar, then all-you-can-eat sushi, and ends up asking about the grill. It is a joke about recognisable formats and our love of abundance, not the history of any real business: it does not imply shared owners, quality or suppliers. And no, Japanese yakiniku and Korean barbecue are not two names for the same thing.

What we ask for is simple: explain what you eat, what it costs and what makes that table special. If the novelty is just that you now do the cooking, we would at least like good instructions.
