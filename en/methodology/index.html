How we work

Four kinds of evidence, always kept apart

Documentary research
Menus, official websites, guides and articles checked on a stated date. It does not mean we ate there.
Our own visit
A meal we paid for, with a receipt. There are none yet.
User reviews
Customer reviews, with platform, relative date and sample size. A small sample is a signal, not a pattern.
Outside award or guide
Recognition from another guide or award, with year, category and recipient. A past award does not prove today’s service.

How we explain a price

  • We show price per person and the total for the group.
  • We separate drinks, bread, dessert, coffee, supplements, minimum portions and children’s costs.
  • We only show what the venue publishes or what reviews report; anything not on record takes up no space on the card.
  • If items are left to add, the total is called a “partial total”, never a “full total”.

How we order recommendations

  • First we rule out known incompatibilities: area, closed at arrival time, base price above budget, outside the city.
  • “No screens” is a hard requirement: places that don’t advertise it are listed separately, with a tip to ask.
  • Then we order by fit with the craving, company and plan; a place we know more about weighs more.
  • Payment, hosting or commission are not part of the formula.

Recommendation rules: 2026-10-25.2

Frank labels

Every label carries a reason, a source and a review date. It expires on its own: if we don’t review it in time, it disappears from the card. None can be bought.

A bargain, with conditions
Clearly cheaper than equivalent alternatives, as long as the conditions we list apply (day, time slot, extras). Price published by the venue and compared with at least one equivalent alternative in the guide. Expires with the price.
Good value
What you eat justifies what you pay, according to recurring reviews and a reasonable published price. Recurring reviews and a published price. After our own visit it becomes our own judgement.
Best on the set menu
The smartest way in is the lunch set menu; à la carte, the bill and the experience change. Set menu published by the venue and reviews that rate it.
You pay for the set
The décor or the location is the main reason to go; the food comes along. It doesn’t mean the food is bad. Reviews where the space clearly outshines the food.
Pricey, but worth it
A high price explained by produce, technique or service. Only after our own visit with a receipt. Not assigned to any venue yet.
Not worth it at this price
There are better equivalent alternatives for the same money, for the stated plan. Our own visit or a documented comparison, and a second review. Not assigned yet.
Repetitive menu
Many dishes that are variations on the same ingredient or sauce. A review of the menu’s ingredients and techniques, not just photos. Not assigned yet.
Would go back for…
The specific dish or plan that justifies going back. Only after our own visit.

What we never invent

  • Visits, tastings or restaurant photos we haven’t done.
  • Taste scores derived from reviews.
  • Prices, hours or children’s facilities that aren’t documented.
  • Walking times: Maps calculates the route.
  • Star ratings in structured data.
  • Criticism made up to look independent.

Valencia city and day trips

Venues in the metro area or on a day trip (such as Fernandet in Sueca, or Hankook in Alfafar) carry their label and are left out of the picker unless you ask for them.
